PROJECT 9 SCIENTIFIC METHODS REVIEW -- d2931d4 -- 2026-11-08
Scope: Research questions, supervision assumptions, and evidence design. Read-only source/document review; no heavy tests, model construction, payload scans, or cluster jobs. Current operational fixes are not re-reported as research defects.

1. INDEPENDENT ACCURACY AND CAUSAL CONTRIBUTION ARE NOT ESTABLISHED BY CONSENSUS AGREEMENT.
Evidence: README.md:7,16-17 specifies ensemble-cleanse -> consensus distillation -> expert validation. code/evaluation/evaluate_multi_model_ensemble.py:534-559 measures model-versus-consensus Dice; :635-641 computes ECE against consensus_mask; :126-165 uses agreement/topology/entropy to assign CLEAN/WEAK/REJECT. :366-382 derives diversity weights from binary foreground disagreement, not measured error against independent truth. docs/SHOWCASE.md:224 explicitly identifies the TotalSeg comparison values as unmatched literature references; :228-243 describes the small expert benchmark. The master plan's goal includes uncertainty/active learning and distillation (../master_research_plan.md:134-137), so selecting presumed quality is part of the scientific intervention.
Confirmed methodological limitation: self-agreement does not identify correctness. Two models can confidently share the same anatomical error; low agreement can also indicate a valuable difficult case rather than label noise. ECE with a consensus target measures confidence versus that target, not calibration against independently annotated anatomy. Architectural diversity does not establish independent errors. Weighting disagreement more heavily can amplify an inaccurate outlier. These are identifiability/counterexample arguments, not measured evidence that every selected label is wrong. The code's software correctness cannot resolve them.
Evidence boundary: current implementation has these surrogate endpoints; the full historical selection benefit, calibration, and student improvement on independent expert anatomy have not been established by this read-only review. SHOWCASE's poor legacy expert ensemble scores are a warning requiring diagnosis, not proof that an updated current pipeline is worse.
Resolving study: blind experts to bucket/model and independently adjudicate a stratified sample spanning CLEAN, WEAK, and REJECT, including tracking of empty/missing organs and difficult anatomy. Estimate genuine label quality and retained-data coverage per bucket, plus calibration/risk-coverage against independent labels. Compare, on identical held-out patients, individual teachers, unweighted majority vote, diversity-weighted vote, and distilled student; ablate selection and ignore masking separately. Match architecture, training budget, training population, and organ ontology. A simpler majority vote or teacher must be a measured internal baseline, not an unrelated literature number. Improvement requires independent expert endpoint gains and not merely higher self-agreement or removing difficult cases.

2. PARTIAL SUPERVISION REQUIRES AN EXPLICIT OBSERVED-LABEL MODEL; IGNORE-255 ALONE DOES NOT PROVIDE ONE.
Evidence: README.md:16 advertises ignoring conflicting boundaries; code/evaluation/hard_threshold_autolabel.py:187-197 starts from human_arr and assigns 255 only where human foreground disagrees with consensus. Human zero remains zero even if consensus predicts an organ there. code/training/asymmetric_loss.py:40-53 constructs a one-hot target and valid_mask excludes only ignore_index; :94-104 implements categorical cross-entropy on all remaining voxels; :126-135 omits absent classes from Dice but does not remove those voxels from categorical CE.
Confirmed code contract: a nonignored zero is supervised as known background. This is valid if zero truly means fully reviewed non-organ tissue. It is not a label-set/marginal partial-supervision likelihood.
Conditional scientific problem: IF a partially annotated/coarse mask uses zero for unannotated organ voxels, CE penalizes correct predictions of those organs as background. Foreground-only disagreement masking does not protect these unknown-background voxels. Conversely, ignoring every disagreement can preferentially remove difficult boundaries and bias retained supervision. Increasing foreground weights or omitting absent classes from Dice does not establish missing-label validity.
Important qualification: this audit does NOT establish which fraction of current training masks have unknown background or assert that all training losses/results are invalid. Label completeness/annotation semantics need an empirical audit; fully labeled masks remain compatible with the existing objective.
Resolving study: record per-case/per-organ annotation completeness and distinguish observed background, positive labels, ignored/unreviewed voxels, and teacher-derived labels. On a fully annotated patient subset, hide known organs/regions in controlled ways and compare current CE+Dice/255 masking, a masked/marginal label-set objective, and complete-label training; evaluate against the untouched complete masks. Separately assess realistic, nonrandom omissions and interreader disagreement. Retain the simpler current loss if the data audit demonstrates fully reviewed backgrounds or if it wins this controlled test.
Primary theoretical source: Gonglei Shi et al., Marginal loss and exclusion loss for partially supervised multi-organ segmentation, arXiv:2007.03868 (2020), https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03868 . Authors explain that background in partial annotations merges true background and unlabelled organs; their marginal loss sums the corresponding probabilities. This supports the conditional distinction above, not a claim that their method will necessarily outperform on this GI cohort.

3. CURRENT EXPERT EVIDENCE AND LEARNING-CURVE DESIGN SUPPORT FEASIBILITY, NOT A GENERAL SCALING LAW OR EXTERNAL CLINICAL VALIDITY.
Evidence: ../master_research_plan.md:209 proposes N=4 -> N=10 using five patients; the plan is explicitly historical/proposed (:6), not live completion evidence. docs/SHOWCASE.md:228-243 reports 11 scans from five patients across six expert NRRD volumes; later-stage rows have one scan/patient. JHU_data_radiologist_corrected/radiologist_clinical_feedback.md:21-35 describes sibling-phase annotation reuse. code/training/run_scaling_sweep.py:634-659 uses supplied gold_standard_cases as static validation and excludes their training-patient overlap; :863-878 selects the best checkpoint by that validation mean. code/evaluation/compute_scaling_law.py:6-7,18-27 fits a three-parameter saturation power law to size-averaged Dice, with no comparison of functional forms or uncertainty on the exponent.
Confirmed design limitations: scans/phases are not additional independent patients; a validation set repeatedly used to choose epochs is a development set, even when training patients are disjoint. Correct grouping and patient-level statistics do not enlarge the underlying five-patient evidence base. A successful three-parameter curve fit does not establish a reproducible scaling law, annotation efficiency, or external transportability. Distinct training scan counts can also reflect phase multiplicity rather than independent anatomical diversity.
Evidence boundary: five selected cases and propagated labels can be useful development/challenge examples. This review does not invalidate descriptive scores or assert that expert-approved reuse is erroneous. It does not verify any larger independent completed external cohort. Propagation needs a separate phase-specific quality check before phase robustness can be claimed.
Resolving study: designate the existing small expert set as development/challenge material. Freeze a patient-level external test cohort before tuning, with independent phase-specific annotation/adjudication checks and prespecified organ/phase endpoints. Build nested development subsets by independent patients and, where practical, measured annotation time; keep test patients, model/pretraining, optimization budget, and inclusion criteria fixed. Run repeated subset draws crossed with training seeds and compare simple learning-curve forms and held-out predictive uncertainty before estimating an exponent. Report patient-count uncertainty, organ-specific failures, and performance across sites/phases. An active-learning claim additionally needs random-sampling and diversity-sampling controls at the same annotation budget. Use a pilot to estimate variance and reader workload before choosing the final sample size; do not infer a powered study from the current five patients.

RECOMMENDED ORDER
First audit label semantics and independently calibrate the CLEAN/WEAK/REJECT buckets. Then run matched baseline/ablation experiments on a frozen patient split. Only after the endpoint and independent cohort are adequate should a scaling-law or annotation-efficiency claim become a primary result. This order addresses the research question before model complexity or additional engineering.
